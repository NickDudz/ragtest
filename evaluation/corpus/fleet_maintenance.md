# Meridian Field Fleet Maintenance Manual

Document ID: MER-FLT-415  
Owner: Fleet Operations  
Revision: 2.8  
Effective date: 2026-01-12

## Scope and responsibilities

This manual covers Meridian Research Cooperative passenger vans, light utility vehicles, and mobile laboratory trailers. Fleet Operations owns preventive maintenance standards and the vehicle register. Drivers perform pre-trip checks and report defects. Site leads schedule access to vehicles, but they may not waive a safety restriction or change a service interval without Fleet Operations approval.

Every vehicle has a unique fleet ID linked to its manufacturer identifier, assigned site, odometer history, service class, warranty status, tire specification, battery type, and approved operators. The register also records equipment mounted to the vehicle. Portable environmental sensors keep their own calibration history and are not calibrated under the vehicle record.

## Pre-trip and post-trip inspection

Before the first trip of a shift, the driver walks around the vehicle and checks tires, visible leaks, lights, mirrors, windows, body damage, doors, and required safety equipment. After starting, the driver checks warning indicators, brakes, steering, horn, fuel or charge level, and any unusual sound. A trailer driver also inspects the hitch, breakaway cable, safety chains, connector, stabilizers, and load restraint.

The driver records the inspection in the fleet application even when no defect is found. If the application is unavailable, a paper card from the glove compartment is used and entered later. A recorded inspection shows that defined observations were made; it is not proof that a hidden component could not fail during the trip.

At the end of a trip, the driver removes waste and personal material, connects shore power when required for mobile laboratory equipment, and records new damage, warning lights, or abnormal handling. Vehicles are returned with at least one-quarter tank or 30 percent traction-battery charge unless an active deployment plan specifies otherwise.

## Preventive service intervals

Light utility vehicles receive preventive service every 8,000 kilometers or six months, whichever occurs first. Service includes fluid and leak checks, brake inspection, tire condition and pressure, steering and suspension inspection, lights, wipers, restraint systems, diagnostic codes, and confirmation of safety equipment. Manufacturer requirements take precedence when they call for a shorter interval.

Passenger vans follow the same six-month maximum but may use a 10,000-kilometer distance interval when the manufacturer permits it. Mobile laboratory trailers receive a chassis and electrical inspection every six months and a brake and bearing service every 12 months. Equipment mounted inside a trailer follows the equipment owner's schedule.

Fleet Operations reviews severe-duty use monthly. Frequent idling, dusty roads, salt exposure, repeated short trips, heavy towing, or operation beyond normal temperature limits may shorten oil, filter, brake, and inspection intervals. The register documents the reason for an adjusted schedule so a later site transfer does not silently keep or discard it.

## Defect classification

A critical defect makes a vehicle unsafe or unlawful to operate. Examples include impaired brakes or steering, exposed tire cord, an active fuel leak, an unsecured traction battery, missing required lights after dark, or a trailer coupling defect. The driver marks the vehicle OUT OF SERVICE, secures the keys, and contacts Fleet Operations. A site lead cannot authorize a one-time trip with a critical defect.

A restricted defect allows movement only under stated conditions, such as travel directly to an approved repair facility in daylight. Fleet Operations records the condition, route, approving mechanic, and expiration. A routine defect does not create immediate safety risk but still receives an owner and target repair date.

Warning lights are evaluated by meaning, not cleared simply to remove a dashboard indication. Diagnostic codes and freeze-frame data are captured before reset when practical. If a warning could affect emissions compliance, braking, steering, restraint systems, or high-voltage safety, Fleet Operations decides disposition before further ordinary use.

## Tires and seasonal readiness

Tires match the approved size, load rating, speed rating, and axle arrangement. Pressure is checked cold against the vehicle placard, not the maximum molded on the tire sidewall. Tread depth is measured at several points, and uneven wear prompts an alignment, suspension, load, or inflation review.

Winter readiness is completed by October 15 for northern sites. It includes cold-rated washer fluid, wiper inspection, battery health, heater and defroster function, emergency blankets, traction aids where approved, and tires appropriate to the site plan. Seasonal readiness supplements rather than replaces the next scheduled preventive service.

After a significant curb strike, pothole impact, or off-road recovery, the driver reports the event even if the tire holds air. Fleet Operations examines the tire, wheel, steering, suspension, and alignment as appropriate. Damage to an internal tire structure may not be visible during the immediate walk-around.

## Starting and traction batteries

Combustion vehicles receive a starting-battery conductance test during preventive service and before winter deployment. Terminals are cleaned and protected according to manufacturer instructions. Repeated jump starts require diagnosis of the battery, charging system, and parasitic draw rather than repeated roadside resets.

Electric-vehicle charging follows the assigned site's electrical plan. Drivers inspect cables and connectors before use and report heat damage, cracked insulation, or a loose receptacle. Routine assignment aims to keep traction batteries between 20 and 90 percent charge; a trip may use a wider range when operationally necessary.

High-voltage battery cases, orange cables, and cooling systems are serviced only by qualified personnel. A collision, water intrusion, thermal warning, or underside strike near the battery triggers isolation guidance from Fleet Operations and emergency services as needed. The vehicle is not stored inside a normal workshop until high-voltage risk has been assessed.

## Fueling, charging, and idle control

Fuel cards are assigned to vehicles, not individuals, and require the current odometer at purchase. Drivers report a lost card immediately. Fuel receipts and charging-session records are reconciled against vehicle location and distance to detect entry errors, leaks, charging faults, or misuse, but an anomaly is investigated before any conclusion about intent.

Unattended idling is avoided except when required for safety, vehicle diagnostics, battery protection, or validated mobile-laboratory loads. Drivers do not use a running vehicle to provide power inside an enclosed loading area. Shore power and auxiliary systems are preferred for stationary laboratory work.

## Telemetry and privacy

Managed vehicles may report location, odometer, fault codes, energy use, and charging status. Fleet Operations uses these data for dispatch, maintenance, incident response, and cost analysis. Routine location data are retained for 12 months. They are not used for continuous productivity scoring or disclosed outside approved operational and legal purposes.

A telemetry gap does not make a vehicle unsafe by itself. Drivers continue pre-trip inspection and manual odometer records while a device is offline. If data return after a gap, automated maintenance reminders are reconciled with the manual record before service is deferred.

## Repair and return to service

Approved repair facilities document complaint, diagnosis, work performed, parts, measurements, diagnostic codes, and test results. Safety-related work receives an independent review when required by law or Fleet Operations policy. Removed parts are retained only when needed for warranty, incident evidence, or technical analysis.

Fleet Operations returns an out-of-service vehicle only after verifying the repair and completing a relevant functional check. The person who requested repair may participate in validation but cannot erase the original defect report. Open routine defects remain visible to drivers until completed or formally accepted with a new due date.

## Records and disposal

Vehicle maintenance records are retained for the life of the vehicle plus five years. Fuel, charging, and routine assignment records are retained for two years unless an incident or legal hold requires longer. When a vehicle is sold, Fleet Operations removes credentials, radios, storage media, markings, toll tags, and site access devices before transfer.
